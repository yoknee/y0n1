How the personas were cut

By accountability, not by seniority alone. The Senior runs the work, the Manager reviews it, the Partner signs it and the Methodology reviewer sets the rules. Each one stands at a different point on the boundary: what they would hand to an agent, what they would never hand off and what would make them trust it. The Associate is covered through contextual inquiry and the anti-persona rather than a card of their own, because in this workflow the Associate executes inside the Senior's plan.

Composite and reconstructed. Each persona is a composite built from the themes in 03. No attribute carries an observation, participant or quote count. Tenure, environment and tools are composite framing, not evidence. Names are invented.

I decided: four composites cut by accountability, not by seniority alone, plus one anti-persona. Role-based views of the same run came out of this cut. We built: the cards from the coded material, with the evidence rows attached, reviewed in critique by the whole design team. Design team of three at this phase.

Dana Whitlock Engagement Senior

Several busy seasons as a Senior. Runs fieldwork on more than one engagement at once. Client site three days a week, laptop on a shared table, calls interrupting. Firm office or home the other two.

GoalsGet every sample traced and every exception explained before the manager's review slot.
Hand the manager a file that needs no re-walk.
Keep the associates moving without redoing their work.
Core tasksDesign the sample and assign items.
Trace samples to evidence, clear the hard ones herself.
Raise and resolve exceptions with the client.
Draft conclusions and prepare for review.
ToolsWorkpaper system, Document request portal, Spreadsheets for tracking, Email and chat with the client
ExpertiseKnows the procedure cold. Knows which client contact answers which request. Does not need guidance; needs speed.
FrustrationsThree systems opened to find one statement.
Review waits, then notes loop.
Re-walking the sample for the manager because the file does not hold the state.
Accountability pressureOwns the fieldwork result. If the file is late or thin, it is her name the manager says first.
Would hand offRetrieval, extraction and the field-by-field matching. 'It can do the matching. I will look at the differences.'
Would never hand offDeciding what an exception means. Talking to the client about it.
What builds trustSeeing the page it read, next to the field it extracted. Being able to rerun one step with the right file.

Rafael Osei Engagement Manager

Manager for several years. Reviews for several engagements in the same weeks. Mostly office or home. Review happens in blocks between client meetings, often late.

GoalsClear review queues before the partner needs the file.
Catch the reference and format problems early so they do not reach the partner.
Know, at a glance, what is waiting on whom.
Core tasksReview workpapers and raise notes.
Judge exception resolutions.
Decide what escalates to the partner.
Coach seniors through notes.
ToolsWorkpaper system review mode, Email for notes outside the system, A personal list of what is waiting (E-24)
ExpertiseReads a workpaper by its references first. Knows which seniors need a second look.
FrustrationsLayout differs per team, so decoding precedes judging.
Notes cleared then reopened.
No view of the whole queue across engagements.

Ingrid Haller Signing Partner

Long-tenured partner. Signs for a portfolio of engagements. Traveling between clients. Reads on a laptop in short windows. Signs when the file is ready, not when it is convenient.

GoalsSign a file she could defend to an inspector a year later.
See the exception trail before the conclusion.
Spend her attention on judgment, not on format.
Core tasksFinal review of high-risk areas.
Decide on misstatements and waivers.
Sign off.
Answer for the file afterward.
ToolsWorkpaper system summary views, The manager's verbal walkthrough
ExpertisePattern-matches across engagements. Knows which exceptions become findings.
FrustrationsThe exception trail is spread across workpapers and emails.
Signing on the manager's walkthrough because the trail is not in the file.
Not knowing what a tool did with the client's data.
Accountability pressurePersonal liability. Independence. 'If it is wrong, it is still my name.'
Would hand offAssembly of the evidence trail. Consistency checks across workpapers.
Would never hand offConclusions. Sign-off. Anything that writes into the file as final.
What builds trustA human checkpoint before anything restricted was read. A visible record of what the agent did, in the file, not in a log.

Tomasz Brenner Quality and Methodology reviewer

Years in audit, then methodology. Writes the rules teams work to. Firm office. Works across engagements, not inside one. Writes the rules teams work to.

GoalsRules that teams can follow without memorizing them.
Structures that fit procedures so teams stop adapting them by hand.
A file that can be re-performed from its contents.
Core tasksMaintain the Test of Details structures.
Set documentation and checkpoint rules.
Review quality findings across engagements.
Answer teams' methodology questions.
ToolsMethodology library, Quality review tooling, Structure templates
ExpertiseKnows every structure and why each field exists. Knows where teams deviate and what it costs.
FrustrationsLocal variants of standard structures.
Data handling rules known unevenly.
Reviewing rules in documents nobody reads under pressure.
Accountability pressureAnswers for methodology compliance across the practice.
Would hand offPlanning a run against a typed structure. Enforcing data classes. Documenting steps consistently.
Would never hand offChanging a structure or a rule without methodology review.
What builds trustThe agent plans against the library, not around it. Checkpoints are rules in the product, not guidance in a document.

Anti-persona: Caleb Marsh, Associate, several busy seasons

Fast, competent, keeps a personal tracker because the systems never held the state he needed. Trusts his own spreadsheet over any tool that slows him down or hides what it did.

Routes around the tool whenAny step that adds a click without adding certainty.
Any output he cannot check in seconds.
Any tool that makes the risk his while keeping the work invisible.
Signals in the productCopies data out to a spreadsheet.
Approves agent output without opening the source.
Reruns the whole run instead of fixing one step.
What the design does about itKeyboard-first density so the product is faster than the spreadsheet (D-15, provisional).
Citation chips that make checking faster than trusting (D-03).
Step-level rejection and rerun so fixing is cheaper than discarding (D-14, provisional).
The run holds the state he used to keep in his head (D-13, provisional).
EvidenceE-04 E-17 E-18 E-21 themes T2, T11, T12, T14

D-13, D-14 and D-15 are provisional register IDs from the plan. Their records are created when artifacts 08, 09 and 11 ship.

What the anti-persona tells the design: the product has to be faster than the workaround and checking has to be cheaper than trusting. A tool that is slower than a spreadsheet or that hides its work gets routed around by exactly the people who could catch its errors. That is Bainbridge's irony in one person.
